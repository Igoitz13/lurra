      What the report contains

      Seven sections, in the order you should read them. Every figure carries its official source and date.

      1. Cadastral record

        Area, use, crops by sub-parcel, and the cadastral area compared with the area measured on the outline.

      2. Terrain

        Elevation, mean and maximum slope, share of the land steeper than 20 % and aspect. In Spain, from the 5 m national terrain model.

      3. Water

        Area of the land inside the 10, 100 and 500-year flood zones, river and coastal, according to Spain's official mapping.

      4. Environmental protection

        Natura 2000 sites and protected natural areas that touch the land, with their name, code and the area affected.

      5. Land use

        SIGPAC land-use units in Spain; a 10 m global land-cover map elsewhere.

      6. Satellite history

        Vegetation vigour month by month since 2017 from Sentinel-2, and the sharp drops that point to felling, fire or building work.

      7. Summary and questions

        A traffic-light summary and the list of questions to take to the town council, the land registry and the seller.

      How it works

      Official data, queried on the spot

      The report is built the moment you ask for it, from the public services of Spain's Catastro, SIGPAC, the Ministry for the Ecological Transition and the National Geographic Institute, plus the Copernicus programme. No stale copies.

      Computed in your browser

      Your browser queries those services directly and does the maths. There is no sign-up, and Lurra neither receives nor stores the reference you look up.

      All of Spain, and a basic report elsewhere

      It works with any parcel in the Spanish cadastre and, with a click on the map, in the Basque Country and Navarre too. Draw a plot in another country and you get terrain, land cover, satellite history and, inside the European Union, Natura 2000.

      What this report is not

      It does not tell you whether you can build: that depends on each municipality's plan and you must ask the town council. It is not a valuation or a land survey either, and it does not replace a land registry extract.

      It is the step before: in a few minutes you know whether the land has an obvious problem and what to ask before paying a deposit or hiring a professional. The result is informative and non-binding.

      Frequently asked questions

      Where do I find the cadastral reference?

      On the property tax (IBI) receipt, on the deed and on the registry extract. If you don't have it, open “Find on the map”, zoom in on the land and click it: the outline comes from the Catastro or from SIGPAC.

      How much does it cost?

      Nothing. The service is in a trial phase and reports are free and unlimited.

      Does it work outside Spain?

      Yes, with fewer layers. Draw the plot or upload a GeoJSON or KML file: you get terrain, land cover and the satellite history anywhere in the world, plus Natura 2000 sites inside the European Union. Flood mapping and cadastral data are only available for Spain.

      If the report says “not in a flood zone”, am I safe?

      Not entirely. The official mapping only covers the river reaches that have been studied. “No” means no mapped flooding over the parcel, not zero risk. When in doubt, ask the river basin authority.

      Why does the satellite part take longer?

      Because your browser reads, month by month, the piece of each Sentinel-2 image that covers the land: between 80 and 200 MB in total, depending on the area, in one or two minutes. The rest of the report appears in seconds, and you can stop the reading at any time: it starts with the most recent years.

      Can I keep the report?

      Yes: “Save as PDF or print” makes your browser produce the PDF. You can also copy the link to generate it again, or download the data.
