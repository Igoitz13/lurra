Agricultural land prices in Spain and Europe

The official average price per hectare of arable land and grassland in each region of Europe and, in Spain, of each type of land in each autonomous community. Hover over a region or tap it to see its price.

Where the data come from

  • Europe: Eurostat, agricultural land prices by region (table apri_lprc), with the latest year published by each region. Where a region does not publish its figure, the national average is used, marked with an asterisk.
  • Spain: Ministerio de Agricultura, Pesca y Alimentación (Spain's Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries and Food), Encuesta de precios de la tierra (land price survey), final 2024 results (base 2020), by autonomous community and land class: rain-fed, irrigated, olive grove, vineyard, fruit orchard, meadow and pasture.
  • Regional boundaries: GISCO, from the European Commission (2021 NUTS regions).

Outside Europe, Lurra reports use the official statistics of the United States (National Agricultural Statistics Service, USDA) and Canada (Statistics Canada), by state or province.

How to read it

  • It is an average: it mixes good and poor plots, large and small. It is not the price of any particular plot.
  • Water makes a big difference to the price: in Spain, a hectare of irrigated land is often worth two or three times as much as a hectare of rain-fed land in the same autonomous community.
  • Countries are not fully comparable: each one measures prices with its own method, and in some the figure includes associated rights or buildings.

The price of a particular plot

The Lurra report works out a reference price for a plot from these same statistics: it takes the type of land of each part of the parcel and its area. It also compares the price in a listing with that reference and warns you if it is well above or well below it. It is still only a guide: what a plot is worth depends on the soil, water, access, buildings and the local market.
