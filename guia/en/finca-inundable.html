How to tell whether a rural plot is prone to flooding

A fertile riverside meadow is, for that very reason, usually a meadow that floods. Knowing this before you buy matters for two reasons: the risk to whatever you put there and the legal limits on building.

What maps exist

In Spain, the reference is the Sistema Nacional de Cartografía de Zonas Inundables (SNCZI), the national flood-zone mapping system, published by the Ministerio para la Transición Ecológica (Spain's environment ministry) using studies by the confederaciones hidrográficas (the river basin authorities). It shows how far the water would reach in three scenarios:

  • 10-year return period (high probability): a flood that occurs, on average, once every ten years.
  • 100 years (medium probability).
  • 500 years (low or exceptional probability). This is the one that water legislation treats as the flood zone.

The return period is an average, not a timetable: a “100-year” flood has a 1% chance of happening in any given year, and it can happen two winters in a row.

How to check

With Lurra, enter the cadastral reference or pick the parcel on the map. The “Water: flood zones” section tells you how many square metres of the plot fall within each zone, for flooding from rivers and from the sea, and draws them on the aerial photo. It also warns you if there is a flood zone less than 100 m away, even if it does not touch the parcel.

If you want to see the full mapping, with water depths and more layers, the ministry has a public SNCZI map viewer.

What the maps do not tell you

Only some stretches of river have been studied: those at greatest risk and those that each confederación hidrográfica has added over time. A small stream, a rambla (a watercourse that is usually dry) or a ravine may not have been studied at all. So a “No” in the report means that there is no mapped flood extent over the parcel, not that the risk is zero.

Look at the land itself as well: if the plot lies on the valley floor, level with the river channel, or the aerial photo shows traces of old river branches, ask questions even if the map is clean. Some regions with their own river basins also publish additional maps.

What the consequences are

  • Control zone (zona de policía). Within 100 m on either side of a watercourse, works and earthmoving need authorisation from the river basin authority. The first 5 m are an easement zone (zona de servidumbre).
  • Preferential flow zone and flood zone. The Reglamento del Dominio Público Hidráulico (the regulation governing public water assets) restricts buildings and certain uses, and the more often an area floods, the stricter the rules.
  • Insurance and value. A plot in a flood zone is not necessarily a bad buy for grazing or crops, but it does constrain farm buildings, houses, fences and other installations.

The exact scope of these restrictions depends on the case. The body that decides is the river basin authority (the confederación hidrográfica or the regional water agency).

What to ask

  • The seller: has water ever come onto the land? How far did it reach?
  • The river basin authority: is the parcel in the control zone, the preferential flow zone or the flood zone? What can be authorised?
  • The town hall: does the local plan record any flood risk on that land?
