Link to Lurra from your website

If your organisation helps people who are looking for, buying or renting land (a farmers' association, a cooperative, a town hall, a land bank or a rural repopulation programme), you are free to link to Lurra: it is free, it does not ask anyone to sign up and it keeps nothing of what is looked up.

The link

The address is https://igoitz13.github.io/lurra/. Each language has its own, for example https://igoitz13.github.io/lurra/eu.html (Basque), https://igoitz13.github.io/lurra/ca.html (Catalan), https://igoitz13.github.io/lurra/gl.html (Galician) or https://igoitz13.github.io/lurra/en/ (English). The full list is at the foot of every page.

Direct links to the report on a parcel

If your website talks about a specific plot (a land bank offer, a parcel for rent or for sale), you can link directly to its report:

  • With the cadastral reference, in Spain: https://igoitz13.github.io/lurra/en/?ref= followed by the 14- or 20-character reference. For example, ?ref=39014A01800003.
  • With a point on the map, in Spain: ?p= followed by the longitude and the latitude separated by a comma, for example ?p=-2.5644,43.0437. Lurra looks for the Catastro or SIGPAC parcel at that point.
  • In any country: draw the plot on the Lurra map (or upload its GeoJSON or KML file), make the report and press “Copy link to this report”. The link carries the outline of the plot and opens its report in any browser.

The report is put together on the spot with that day's public data, so it is always up to date.

The logo

You can use the Lurra icon alongside the link: icono-512.png (512 × 512 px) or icono.svg.

Before you link

Lurra is a personal project. Its reports are informative and non-binding: they do not replace the nota simple (the Land Registry extract), the town hall or a qualified professional. If you have questions or a proposal, write to .
