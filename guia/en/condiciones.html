The service

Lurra produces reports on rural parcels from public data. It is free and does not require registration. By using it you accept these terms.

Informative and non-binding

The report summarises what the sources say on the date it is generated. It is not official mapping, a certification, a valuation, a topographic survey or a planning report. It does not say whether you can build or which uses are permitted: that depends on the municipal plan and on the rules of each autonomous community.

Limits worth knowing

  • Flooding. Official mapping covers only the stretches of river and coast that have been studied. If the report finds no flood zone, that does not mean the risk is zero. Outside Spain a global model is used, with cells of about 150 m, which considers only large rivers and does not take flood defences into account: it is a first indication and does not replace each country's official maps.
  • Environmental protection and public land. The report queries Natura 2000, protected natural areas, livestock trails and public forests from the national inventories. Other designations are not included, such as the public water domain or coastal public land, or the protections in the municipal plan. The inventories may be incomplete: some autonomous communities do not have their livestock trails in the national network, which in any case gives the centre line of each trail and not its surveyed width. Outside Spain the global Protected Planet database is used, which may be missing some sites and whose outlines may be approximate.
  • Planning. The land classification comes from the Sistema de Información Urbana, a national summary of the planning data that municipalities report: it may be out of date, it does not distinguish the categories of rural land (ordinary or protected) and it does not say what can be built. Only the town hall can certify that.
  • Areas. They are measured on the digital outline. They may differ from the cadastral area, the registered area and the actual area.
  • Terrain. In Spain the IGN's 5 m model is used. Elsewhere, a global model of about 30 m: on small plots the slope is only indicative.
  • Satellite. The images have a resolution of 10 to 20 m and some months have no data because of clouds. A drop in vegetation vigour is a sign that needs checking, not proof.
  • SIGPAC. It is a tool for managing farm subsidies. It does not prove ownership or the land classification.
  • Soil, access and groundwater. The soil type comes from a global model at 250 m and is no substitute for a soil test. Tracks and services come from OpenStreetMap, which is made by volunteers: some may be missing, and a track on the map does not guarantee a right of way. The groundwater status is that of the whole groundwater body, not of a particular well.
  • Comparison with the listing. It compares what you enter with the public data in the report. It helps you prepare your questions, but it does not tell you whether a plot is a good or a bad buy.
  • Wildfires. The report uses burned-area perimeters detected by satellite (Copernicus): in Europe since 2000 and for fires of about 30 ha or more; in the rest of the world, since 2012. Small fires may not appear, and the perimeters are approximate.
  • Land price. It is the average price published by the official statistics for that type of land in the region, not a valuation of the plot. The actual price depends on soil quality, water, access, buildings and the local market. Outside the European Union, the United States and Canada there is no comparable statistic, and the report does not show it.

Availability

Lurra depends on third-party services that may fail, change or stop being available without notice. When a source does not respond, the report says so and leaves that section without data. There is no guarantee that the service will always work or that it will continue to exist.

Liability

The service is provided as is. To the extent permitted by law, its author is not liable for any damage or loss that may result from decisions made on the basis of a report. Before signing, paying a deposit or investing, check the information with the Registro de la Propiedad (the Land Registry), the town hall, the river basin authority and a qualified professional.

Reasonable use

Queries are sent from your browser to public services that have their own terms. Do not use Lurra for bulk or automated downloads, or in any way that could harm those services.

Changes

The service is in a testing phase. These terms and the service itself may change. If paid features are added in the future, they will be clearly announced before they are activated, and nothing will ever be charged without your express consent.

The website and the reports are available in several languages. Except for Spanish and English, they are machine translations, which may contain errors. These terms, the legal notice and the privacy page were drafted in Spanish; the other versions are translations and, in the event of any discrepancy, the Spanish version prevails.
