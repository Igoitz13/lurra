How to find the cadastral reference of a rural plot

The cadastral reference is the official identifier of each property in the Catastro (Spain's property register for tax purposes). It pinpoints the parcel without ambiguity, and it is what Lurra asks for to make the report.

Where it appears

  • On the IBI (property tax) bill, which many people still call “la contribución”.
  • On the deed of sale or the deed of inheritance.
  • Usually, on the nota simple (an extract from the Registro de la Propiedad, the Land Registry).
  • In many sale listings. If it is not there, ask the seller or the estate agent for it: they have no reason to refuse.

If you do not have it

There are three free routes:

  • In Lurra, with the polygon and parcel: on the “Cadastral reference (Spain)” tab, open “No reference? Search by polygon and parcel number”, choose the province and the municipality and enter the polygon and parcel numbers shown on the deed or the IBI bill. Lurra asks the Catastro for the reference and prepares the report.
  • In Lurra, on the map: open the “Pick a parcel (Spain)” tab, search for the municipality or the place name, zoom in until you see the parcels and click on the plot. The outline and the reference come from the Catastro.
  • On the Sede Electrónica del Catastro (the Catastro's official website): the property search lets you find a rural parcel by province, municipality, polygon and parcel, and the map viewer lets you look for it on the map.

How to read it

The full reference has 20 characters. For a rural plot, the first 14 identify the parcel. For example, 39014A01800003:

  • 39: the province (Cantabria).
  • 014: the municipality.
  • A: the sector.
  • 018: the polygon.
  • 00003: the parcel.

The remaining six characters distinguish the properties within the parcel and serve as check characters. The first 14 are enough for the report, although you can paste all 20.

“Polygon 18, parcel 3” is how the same plot is referred to in the village, in the deed and in SIGPAC (the official map of farm parcels), so with the municipality, the polygon and the parcel you can reconstruct the reference.

Navarre and the Basque Country

Navarre, Álava, Bizkaia and Gipuzkoa have their own cadastres, with references in a different format that are not in the national Catastro. In Lurra you can also search for them by polygon and parcel: choose Álava/Araba, Bizkaia, Gipuzkoa or Navarra in the list of provinces, and the report takes the outline from their foral cadastre (the regional cadastre). If you click on the plot on the map, it takes the SIGPAC land-use unit, which covers the whole of Spain.

A word of warning

The Catastro is a tax register. Its areas and boundaries are a good approximation, but they do not prove ownership or fix the legal boundaries of the plot: that is a matter for the Land Registry and, in case of dispute, for a formal boundary survey (deslinde).
