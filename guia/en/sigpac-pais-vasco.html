SIGPAC viewer Euskadi: access and how to find a parcel

SIGPAC (Sistema de Información Geográfica de Parcelas Agrícolas) is Spain's official map of agricultural parcels: it shows the use of each piece of a plot, its slope, its area and whether it is irrigated. In the Basque Country (Álava, Bizkaia and Gipuzkoa) it is maintained by the Departamento de Alimentación, Desarrollo Rural, Agricultura y Pesca of the Gobierno Vasco (the Basque Government), and you can look it up online for free.

Open the official viewer or make your plot's report in Lurra

Where to look it up

  • SIGPAC, visor cartográfico de Euskadi (the Basque SIGPAC map viewer), from the Departamento de Alimentación, Desarrollo Rural, Agricultura y Pesca of the Gobierno Vasco: sigpac.nekanet.net/sigpac.
  • Ministry of Agriculture's SIGPAC viewer, which covers the whole of Spain: sigpac.mapa.gob.es.
  • Lurra: enter the cadastral reference or search by municipality, polygon and parcel, and the report shows the SIGPAC land-use units with their use, slope and irrigation, alongside the Catastro, flood zones, protected areas, wildfires, the reference land price and the satellite history.

The Basque SIGPAC was drawn up from the rural cadastre of each province, but the Gobierno Vasco warns that changing a parcel in SIGPAC does not change the cadastre. The cadastre is run by each diputación foral (provincial government), each with its own viewer: Álava (araba.eus), Bizkaia (bizkaia.eus) and Gipuzkoa (gipuzkoa.eus).

How to find a parcel

  1. Choose the province and the municipality.
  2. Enter the polygon number and the parcel number. They appear on the deed, on the nota simple (the Land Registry extract) and on the bills and certificates of the foral cadastre (the cadastre run by each Basque province).
  3. Click on each land-use unit to see its use, area, slope and irrigation coefficient.

What to check before buying

  • The use of each unit: if the listing talks about arable land and SIGPAC says shrub pasture or forest, something does not add up.
  • Irrigation: the coefficient tells you whether the administration recognises the unit as irrigable, but the right to the water has to be checked with the irrigation community (comunidad de regantes) or the confederación hidrográfica (the river basin authority).
  • Usable area: roads and tracks, buildings, water and unproductive land do not count, and the sum of the productive units may be considerably smaller than the area of the parcel.
  • Mean slope: a guide to how much of the land can be worked with machinery.

SIGPAC does not prove ownership or say what can be built. The guide What SIGPAC is explains all the uses and what SIGPAC does not tell you.
