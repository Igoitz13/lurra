SIGPAC viewer Illes Balears: access and how to find a parcel

SIGPAC (Sistema de Información Geográfica de Parcelas Agrícolas) is Spain's official map of agricultural parcels: it shows the use of each piece of a plot, its slope, its area and whether it is irrigated. In the Balearic Islands (Mallorca, Menorca, Ibiza and Formentera) it is maintained by FOGAIBA (Fondo de Garantía Agraria y Pesquera de las Illes Balears, the islands' agricultural and fisheries guarantee fund), part of the Conselleria de Agricultura, Pesca y Medio Natural (the regional agriculture department), and you can look it up online for free.

Open the official viewer or make your plot's report in Lurra

Where to look it up

  • Visor de la IDEIB (Infraestructura de Datos Espaciales de las Illes Balears), the viewer of the Balearic spatial data infrastructure, run by FOGAIBA (Fondo de Garantía Agraria y Pesquera de las Illes Balears) of the Conselleria de Agricultura, Pesca y Medio Natural: ideib.caib.es/visor.
  • Ministry of Agriculture's SIGPAC viewer, which covers the whole of Spain: sigpac.mapa.gob.es.
  • Lurra: enter the cadastral reference or search by municipality, polygon and parcel, and the report shows the SIGPAC land-use units with their use, slope and irrigation, alongside the Catastro, flood zones, protected areas, wildfires, the reference land price and the satellite history.

IDEIB also has a “SIGPAC por años” (SIGPAC by year) viewer for seeing what a land-use unit looked like in earlier campaigns. Requests to amend SIGPAC can only be submitted electronically, on the Govern's e-government site (the Balearic regional government) and addressed to FOGAIBA, by 15 November each year; if there is no reply within six months, they are deemed to have been rejected.

How to find a parcel

  1. Choose the province and the municipality.
  2. Enter the polygon number and the parcel number. They appear on the deed, on the nota simple (the Land Registry extract) and on the IBI (property tax) bill.
  3. Click on each land-use unit to see its use, area, slope and irrigation coefficient.

What to check before buying

  • The use of each unit: if the listing talks about arable land and SIGPAC says shrub pasture or forest, something does not add up.
  • Irrigation: the coefficient tells you whether the administration recognises the unit as irrigable, but the right to the water has to be checked with the irrigation community (comunidad de regantes) or the confederación hidrográfica (the river basin authority).
  • Usable area: roads and tracks, buildings, water and unproductive land do not count, and the sum of the productive units may be considerably smaller than the area of the parcel.
  • Mean slope: a guide to how much of the land can be worked with machinery.

SIGPAC does not prove ownership or say what can be built. The guide What SIGPAC is explains all the uses and what SIGPAC does not tell you.
