Legal distances to rivers, roads and the coast on rural land

A plot next to a stream, a road or the sea, or under a power line, has strips where you cannot do just anything. It is worth knowing before you buy, especially if you plan to fence, build or open an access.

Rivers and streams

The Ley de Aguas (the Spanish Water Act) protects both banks of any watercourse, including streams that only carry water in winter:

  • Easement zone: 5 m from the edge of the channel. It is a public right of way for surveillance, fishing and rescue. You cannot build on it or close it off with a fence that blocks the way.
  • Control zone: 100 m from the edge of the channel. Works, buildings, fences, earthmoving, extraction of sand and gravel and tree felling need authorisation from the river basin authority (the confederación hidrográfica or the regional water agency).

If the plot is also in a flood zone, the restrictions are greater. The guide How to tell whether a rural plot is prone to flooding explains them.

Roads

For state roads, the Ley de Carreteras (the Spanish Roads Act) sets strips on each side, measured from the outer edge of the road's earthworks:

  • Motorways, dual carriageways and expressways: public domain of 8 m, easement zone of 25 m, road protection zone of 100 m and building line at 50 m.
  • Ordinary roads: public domain of 3 m, easement zone of 8 m, road protection zone of 50 m and building line at 25 m.

You cannot build between the road and the building line. In the road protection zone, works, changes of use, fences and new accesses need authorisation from the road owner. Roads belonging to the autonomous communities and the provincial councils (diputaciones) have their own laws, with similar distances: ask the owner of yours.

Coast

The Ley de Costas (the Spanish Coasts Act) protects the strip next to the sea, measured from the inland edge of the shore:

  • Right-of-way easement: 6 m, kept free for public passage.
  • Protection easement: 100 m on rural land. Houses and other buildings are prohibited; what already existed is subject to special rules.
  • Influence zone: 500 m, where planning limits building density.

The exact line is set by the official boundary survey (deslinde) of the maritime-terrestrial public domain. Ask the Costas (coastal authority) office in your province whether it has been approved and where it runs.

High-voltage power lines

A line crossing the plot usually has a right-of-way easement, either registered or created by compulsory purchase. You cannot build under the line, and to build or plant trees nearby you must respect the safety distances in the high-voltage lines regulation, which depend on the line's voltage. The company that owns the line will tell you how wide the easement is.

How to check your plot

The Lurra report includes the “Protection strips” table. It gives the distance from the edge of the parcel to the nearest river or stream, motorway, road, coast and high-voltage line, says which strip the plot falls in and whom to ask. The distances come from OpenStreetMap and are measured to the centre line drawn on the map. The law measures from the edge of the channel, the road or the shore, so the real strip reaches a little further: treat it as a warning and confirm the figure with the authority.

What to ask

  • The river basin authority: what can I do in the control zone, and what authorisation do I need?
  • The road owner: where is the building line? Can I open an access?
  • The Costas office: has the public domain boundary been surveyed? What part of the plot is in the protection easement?
  • The electricity company and the Land Registry: what easement does the line have?
