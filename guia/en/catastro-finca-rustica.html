The Catastro for a rural plot: how to look it up for free and what data it gives

The Catastro (the Spanish cadastre) is the inventory of all properties in Spain for tax purposes. For a rural plot, it tells you where it is, what shape and area it has and what crops are grown on it. Almost all of it can be looked up for free, without being the owner.

Where to look it up

  • On the Sede Electrónica del Catastro (the Catastro's official website). The cadastral data search lets you find a parcel by its cadastral reference or by province, municipality, polygon and parcel, and its map viewer lets you find it on the map. Without identifying yourself you see the public data; for the protected data you have to be the owner or prove an interest, using a digital certificate or Cl@ve (the Spanish government's electronic identification system).
  • On Lurra. Enter the cadastral reference, search by municipality, polygon and parcel or, in the “Pick a parcel (Spain)” tab, click on the parcel. The report gathers the public Catastro data and adds what the Catastro does not tell you: flood zones, protected areas, slope, SIGPAC use (the land use recorded for farm subsidies) and satellite history.

If you do not have the reference, the guide to the cadastral reference explains where to find it and how to read it.

Which data are public

  • Cadastral reference: the parcel identifier, 20 characters long (for a rural plot, the first 14 identify the parcel).
  • Location: municipality, polygon, parcel and, often, the local place name (paraje).
  • Area of the parcel according to the cadastral maps.
  • Class and use: rural, and the main use (agricultural, forestry…).
  • Sub-parcels: the parts of the parcel with a different crop or use, each with its own area.
  • Buildings, if any have been declared, with their use and floor area.
  • Maps: the outline of the parcel on the map and the neighbouring parcels.

The owner's name and the cadastral value are protected. That is why they do not appear in the Lurra report. The guide to the cadastral value explains who can see it and what it is used for.

How to read the crops

Each sub-parcel carries a crop or land-use code and a productive intensity, which form the basis of the cadastral value of rural land. Some common codes:

  • C-, rain-fed arable land; CR, irrigated arable land.
  • E-, pasture; MT, scrub.
  • O-, rain-fed olive grove.
  • I-, unproductive (rocky ground, tracks, barren land with no use).

These crops are updated from time to time and may not match what is on the ground today. Compare them with the use in SIGPAC, which is reviewed more often against aerial photos, and with the satellite image.

What the Catastro does not tell you

  • Who the legal owner is: that is shown by the Registro de la Propiedad (the Land Registry). The Catastro is a tax register, and its area may not match the registered one. The guide to the nota simple explains this.
  • What can be built: a parcel being listed as rural in the Catastro does not tell you what the municipal plan allows. See the guide Can you build on a rural plot?
  • Whether a building is legal: appearing in the Catastro is not the same as having a licence.
  • Risks and protections: flooding, Natura 2000, public forests and drovers' roads are not part of the cadastral record.

Navarre and the Basque Country

Navarre, Álava, Bizkaia and Gipuzkoa have their own cadastres, run by the Government of Navarre and by each diputación foral (provincial government), each with its own online search. Their parcels are not in the national Catastro. In Lurra you can search for them by municipality, polygon and parcel, choosing the territory in the list of provinces: the report takes the outline from the foral cadastre. If you click on the plot on the map, it takes the SIGPAC land-use unit.
